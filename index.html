Evidence first. Conclusions second.

A diagnosis is not a coverage decision.

Knowing what's wrong with a vehicle doesn't always tell you whether the repair is covered. I look through the repair records, coverage rules, and past messages to help owners see what's been decided and what questions still need answers.

Three questions. Three different answers.

01

What was found?

What did the technician observe, and which tests and records support the recommended repair?

02

What does the program cover?

What problem does the program cover, and do the repair records show this vehicle has it?

03

What did the manufacturer decide?

Program coverage and goodwill are separate answers, and a case number is not an approval.

The work

Follow the record.
Not the assumption.

The aim is a question specific enough to answer—and an answer that can be checked against the source.

  1. 01

    Read the original terms.

    Identify the applicable published conditions and technical instructions. A general component failure does not automatically establish eligibility.

  2. 02

    Trace the evidence.

    Review repair orders, technician notes, tests and available inspection records. Keep observed facts separate from interpretations.

  3. 03

    Identify what is missing.

    Ask which criterion was evaluated, which record supports the answer and which details remain unresolved.

  4. 04

    Clarify the written terms.

    Separate program determinations from goodwill, and document any additional testing, repair scope, owner cost, exclusions or dates before the owner decides.
