Evidence first. Conclusions second.

A diagnosis is not a coverage decision.

Knowing what's wrong with a vehicle doesn't always tell you whether the repair is covered. I look through the repair records, coverage rules, and past messages to help owners see what's been decided and what questions still need answers.

Three questions. Three different answers.

01

What was found?

What did the technician observe, and which tests and records support the recommended repair?

02

What does the program cover?

What problem does the program cover, and do the repair records show this vehicle has it?

03

What did the manufacturer decide?

Program coverage and goodwill are separate answers, and a case number is not an approval.

Start with the records.

I use the records to ask clear questions and check the answers.

  1. 01

    Check the program rules.

    Read what the program covers and how the repair should be checked. A failed part alone doesn't mean the repair qualifies.

  2. 02

    Review the repair records.

    Look at repair orders, technician notes, test results, and inspection records. Keep the findings separate from assumptions.

  3. 03

    Find the unanswered questions.

    Check which requirements were reviewed, what records support the answer, and what still needs explaining.

  4. 04

    Get the details in writing.

    Keep coverage decisions separate from possible goodwill. Confirm any further testing, repair work, costs, exclusions, and deadlines before the owner decides.
