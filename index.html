The methodBackground

A diagnosis is not a coverage decision.

A repair estimate can say what needs replacing without showing whether a manufacturer’s published coverage criteria are met. I compare those criteria with the diagnostic record, distinguish program coverage from possible goodwill, and identify what still needs a written answer. The owner decides what happens next.

Specific criteria. Documented findings. Clear decisions.

Who answers what.

  1. The dealer documents

    Records the condition observed, testing performed, and basis of the repair recommendation.

  2. The manufacturer reviews

    Considers the program criteria and, separately, whether goodwill assistance is available.

  3. The owner decides

    Receives the written information and alone decides what work, costs, or offers to accept.

A diagnosis, a program determination, and an offer of assistance are three different things. Each deserves its own documented answer.

The method

  1. Read the actual terms

    Begin with the published manufacturer program and its technical instructions. A program may describe a specific condition, not every failure involving the same part.

  2. Identify what was observed

    Review the repair order, technician notes, diagnostic results, road-test findings, and available inspection records. Distinguish recorded observations from interpretations and gaps.

  3. Ask for the basis

    Which published criterion was evaluated? What evidence supports the determination? If a required test or record is missing, identify it rather than guessing.

  4. Keep the decisions distinct

    Program coverage and discretionary goodwill are separate reviews. A customer-support reference number confirms a record exists; it does not confirm repair approval.

  5. Clarify work and cost

    Before further inspection or repair, the owner reviews the scope, proposed testing, possible fees, written offer, exclusions, and relevant dates. The owner decides whether to proceed.

What counts as evidence.

Four kinds of information stay distinct: what the owner reports; what the dealer documented; what the manufacturer published or decided; and what independent research identified. An unanswered question is never treated as an established fact.

The work stays private. No owner, dealer, employee, correspondence, repair history, or outcome is identified or discussed publicly. Every outgoing message is written by the owner, in the owner’s own words. The owner is visibly included in email, receives responses, and makes the decisions. Communication is by email only.
