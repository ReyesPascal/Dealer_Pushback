What gets checked

  • Published conditions

    A manufacturer program can cover a specific condition, not every repair involving the same component. I compare the current published program and technical instructions with the vehicle-specific facts; I never treat a broad diagnosis as proof of eligibility.

  • Dealer evidence

    The repair order, technician notes, diagnostic observations, test results and any available inspection records show what was actually checked. Missing information is identified as missing, not filled in with assumptions.

  • Manufacturer review

    Coverage under a published program and possible goodwill assistance are separate questions. A tracking number is not an approval. The manufacturer’s written answer should make clear what it decided and the basis for that answer.

  • Scope, cost and timing

    Before any additional inspection or repair, the owner should know the proposed work and any charge. A written offer should identify what is included, what the owner would pay, its conditions, and any relevant estimate-expiration date.

  • The owner’s words

    Every outgoing message is written by the owner, in her own words. The owner is visibly copied, receives the answers, and decides whether to approve work or accept any offer.* Communication is by email only.

Three distinct responsibilities.

  1. The dealer documents

    What condition was observed, what tests were completed, and which specific requirement was or was not met?

  2. The manufacturer reviews

    Does the published program apply? If it does not, is separate goodwill assistance available, and on what written terms?

  3. The owner decides

    What work, if any, to approve; what costs to accept; and how to proceed after reading the actual written answers.

A clear record does not guarantee coverage or assistance. It makes the questions, answers and remaining unknowns visible.
